## The one missing line

`UnitCard` has carried a `tribe` since the first prototype. `makeUnit` never copied it onto the `Entity`. So the reason race was decoration was not that no card wanted a tribal trait — it was that nothing on the board had a race to read. Everything else followed from adding that field.

## What was believed and proved false

**That a hero counting kin was harmless.** The rule was written as "a hero is never counted" — `adjacentAllies` skips `isHero`, so a Kindle standing rightmost sees only its left neighbour. What was not noticed is that the rule only ran in one direction. A hero *asking* `adjacentKin(hero, 'different')` got the unit on its left, because that unit is not of race `'hero'`. A hero carrying Banner would have taken +1 every turn, for standing at the right end of the line rather than for any race. It was found by an assertion in the new test file that happened to ask the question both ways round, not by review, and the fix is one line in `adjacentKinOf`.

**That the panel could absorb a longer sentence.** `RACE_HAS_NO_RULE` was 175 characters and the panel was 266px against a 270px band — `src/render/glossary.ts` says so, with the measurement. `RACE_RULE`'s first draft was 255 characters, which wrapped to a third line and made the panel 280px; the area of the player's row it covered went from 2,341px² to 11,623px². `npm test` was green throughout, because the length gate read `xp__gloss` and the paragraph that grew was `xp__note`. `npm run probe:ui hover 7 even light` is what said so, run because `docs/policies/local-rules.md` requires it after a change to a screen. The A/B was controlled: the old sentence was put back, the probe re-run, 266px and 2,341px² came back, then the new sentence was shortened to 179 characters and confirmed at 266px. The gate now reads both paragraphs and asserts neither is empty.

**That "the count is read at apply-time rather than spawn-time" was a gate.** It was written in the resolver's comment and a test claimed to hold it. The mutation — freeze the count at the spawn site, three coordinated edits — left the whole suite green, and had to: `act` queues `tribePower` immediately ahead of the swing, so both sites read the same board. The comment now says there is no gate and that the choice is made on the general rule. The test was rewritten around the `alive` guard, which is falsifiable.

## Two mutations that came back green and were right to

The first draft of the union mutation deleted `'banner'` from `TribalTrait` and left the runtime record alone. Node strips types, so `Object.keys(TRIBAL)` still held `'banner'` and nothing moved. That is the bound on the type-level half of this design: **the compiler catches a union and its record disagreeing, and cannot catch a trait that was never added to the union.** The behavioural gate is for the second shape, so the mutation was rewritten as it — Relay given `&& r.tribe !== e.tribe`, which compiles cleanly and goes red on "exactly the traits in TRIBAL_TRAITS notice a neighbour's race".

The other was the apply-time claim above.

## The numbers

`npm run verify` is **byte-for-byte unchanged**: A optimal 66.25%, B random 56.50%, gap 9.75 pp (CI 5.61..13.89). `PLAYER_DECK` is the measured deck, no tribal card is in it, and the engine addition is inert on a board with no tribal trait on it. That is the strongest single piece of evidence that the change is additive.

`npm run verify:run` moved, at 200 seeds: greedy/search 88.00% → 89.00%, random/search 54.50% → 49.50%, greedy/right 77.50% → 77.00%, random/right 36.50% → 32.50%. **These are not the same runs with tribal cards added.** `docs/policies/local-rules.md` already records why: a `RunLog` is a list of indices into offers, so six new pool entries make seed *n* a different run from its first reward node onward. Re-measured at 1000 seeds on the new content — 88.80%, 49.40%, 75.10%, 32.60% — every point estimate lands inside the base revision's own 200-seed interval.

What moved is the shelf, not the cards. A probe over 200 seeds a class, driving `runRun` through `makeRunAgent`, found finished decks holding **0.43** tribal cards per run as a Knight, **0.77** as a Ranger and **0.35** as a Mage, out of 21–29 cards. At that density the cards cannot be carrying a win rate. `cardValue` in `src/sim/runbots.ts` cannot see a race, so it prices a Kindler as a 1/4 for 2 — and the two most-drafted of the six are `u_marshal` and `u_runesmith`, the 3-cost bodies it *can* price. The pool got wider and the bot got no better at choosing from it. Per the owner's ruling that is information about the bot, not a reason to re-cost anything.

The two golden format 1 logs were untouched by any of this, because `test/sigils.test.ts` pins them against `LEGACY_REWARDS` rather than the live table. That pin is the standing cost `docs/policies/local-rules.md` describes, and this round is the second content change it has absorbed.
